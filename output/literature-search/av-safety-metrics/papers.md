## Summary

- **Closest-work clusters**: (1) classical analytical surrogate safety measures (SSM) — TTC/PET/DRAC family and their generalized/geometry-aware descendants; (2) formal rule-based safety envelopes (RSS and successors); (3) learned/data-driven risk models trained on naturalistic (non-crash) data — the cluster CARM sits in, and where the two nearest competitors (GSSM, NeuralMetric) live.
- **Opportunity map**: the rule-based side is a mature, well-surveyed field (Westhofen et al. 2022 is the canonical taxonomy); the learning-based side is young and fast-moving — two papers from 2025/2026 (GSSM, NeuralMetric) attack almost the same problem statement as CARM ("outcome-agnostic, no crash labels needed, trained on naturalistic logs") and must be read in full and distinguished explicitly, not just cited in passing.
- **Strongest baselines already in your codebase**: TTC (raw + geometry-aware 3D), PET, DRAC — all correctly identified as the standard rule-based comparison set (`ssm_baselines.py`, `evaluation.py`).
- **Benchmark/dataset candidates**: DeepAccident, Bench2Drive — both already adapters in this repo; both have citable primary papers.
- **Novelty risks**: GSSM (Jiao et al., Nature Machine Intelligence 2026) and NeuralMetric (ScienceDirect 2026) are the two papers most likely to be raised by a reviewer as "why not just use this." Positioning notes below.
- **Recommended next action**: read GSSM and NeuralMetric in full (not abstract-only, as done here) before finalizing the related-work section and the novelty claim; hand this packet to `ccf-paper-writer` for the actual Related Work prose once that reading is done.

## Clusters

### Cluster 1: Classical/geometric surrogate safety measures (rule-based, deterministic)

- Representative papers: #1 Hayward (TTC), #2 Allen et al. (PET), #3 SSAM, #7 generic 3D-SSM framework, #8 TTCmo.
- What this cluster already solves: closed-form, interpretable, no-training-data-needed conflict indicators; well validated against real crash statistics over 50 years; SSAM packages them into a usable tool.
- Remaining gap: single-frame or short-horizon kinematic snapshots, brittle under occlusion/uncertainty, no learned context-adaptivity (same interaction type is treated identically regardless of agent class, road geometry, or population-level base rates), and — per #7/#8 — the classic 1D formulation is measurably wrong (up to 300%) versus a geometry-aware 3D treatment. Persistence ("how long was risk sustained," not just peak) is essentially unaddressed in this cluster.
- Possible rescue/differentiation route (already taken in this project): use the analytic SSM as a *physically-grounded prior* (`safety_prior.py`'s `tau_phy`), not the final answer, and let a learned model produce a bounded, context-adaptive residual correction (CARM's `[0.5x, 2x]` bound) plus a persistence-aware evaluation on top.
- How it affects your paper: cite #4 (Westhofen survey) once as "the field is mature and fragmented"; cite #7 directly and specifically since your `geometry.py` 3D-TTC implements essentially the idea their paper argues for — you should state whether your formulation matches, extends, or independently arrived at the same generalization.

### Cluster 2: Formal rule-based safety envelopes (RSS family)

- Representative papers: #6 RSS.
- What this cluster already solves: a provable, worst-case safe-distance envelope with legal/responsibility semantics — good for certification-style arguments, not for graded severity comparison across trajectories.
- Remaining gap: binary/threshold safe-unsafe judgment per rule, not a continuous, calibrated risk score; doesn't naturally support "how close AND how persistently" (your S3.1 framing) or ranking two both-safe trajectories by relative margin.
- Possible rescue/differentiation route: one clear related-work sentence contrasting CARM's continuous, calibrated `R(t)` against RSS's binary compliance check — different use case (formal verification vs. post-hoc severity scoring), not a strictly better/worse comparison.
- How it affects your paper: low risk to novelty (different problem framing), but expected in any ICRA safety-metrics related work section — omitting RSS entirely would look like a gap to a reviewer familiar with the AV-safety-verification literature.

### Cluster 3: Learned / data-driven risk models trained on naturalistic (non-crash-labeled) data

- Representative papers: #10 GSSM, #11 RiskNet, #12 NeuralMetric, #13/#14 calibrated-analytic risk fields, #15 probabilistic risk-spot detector, #16 reachability+learned hybrid.
- What this cluster already solves: context-adaptive, learned severity scoring that doesn't require exhaustive crash labels; several report AUROC/AUPRC and early-warning "lead time" evaluation protocols nearly identical in spirit to `evaluation.py`'s metrics.
- Remaining gap: **#10 (GSSM) and #12 (NeuralMetric) are the two papers structurally closest to CARM's stated contribution** — both learned, both outcome-agnostic/naturalistic-trained, both evaluated with AUPRC + timeliness. Neither (per what's extractable from abstracts/snippets here) is confirmed to (a) explicitly compose a physically-grounded analytic prior with a bounded learned residual the way CARM does, (b) report persistence metrics (risk-time-integral, critical duration) rather than only detection accuracy/lead-time, or (c) do per-interaction-type/per-object-class conditioning the way your `interaction.py`/class-aware `tau_phy` do — but this is inferred from partial access, not confirmed absence, and must be checked by actually reading both papers before claiming it in a submission.
- Possible rescue/differentiation route: (1) the analytic-prior-plus-bounded-residual composition (interpretability + a hard bound on how far the network can override physics) as opposed to a more end-to-end learned score; (2) the persistence-metric contribution (Section "Extensions beyond the base spec" in your own README) as a genuinely distinct evaluation-side contribution neither GSSM nor NeuralMetric appears to report; (3) cross-domain generalization results (Bench2Drive→DeepAccident zero-shot) as an evidence axis.
- How it affects your paper: **highest-priority reading task before the related-work section is final.** Do not cite GSSM/NeuralMetric from abstracts alone in the actual manuscript — pull full text, compare method diagrams and metric tables directly, and write the differentiation sentence only after that.

## Reproducibility Notes (added after user follow-up, 2026-09-08)

Checked whether #11 (RiskNet) and #13 (Driver's Risk Field) could realistically be reproduced/reimplemented as extra baselines for the CARM comparison.

**#13 Driver's Risk Field (Kolekar et al. 2020) — high feasibility, low effort.**
Closed-form field (modified torus, Gaussian cross-section): `z(x,y) = a·exp[-((x-xc)²+(y-yc)²-Rcar²)/(2σ²)]` with `a(s)=p(s-v·tla)²` and per-side widths `σᵢ=m+kᵢ|δ|·s+c`; risk = field × environmental cost map, summed over grid. All parameters have published fitted values (Tables 1-3 of the paper: `p=0.0064, tla=3.5s, m=0.001, k1=0, k2=1.3823, c=0.5`, plus driver-model and environment-cost constants). Authors released **MATLAB code + fitted parameters + experimental/simulation data on 4TU.ResearchData** (DOI `10.4121/uuid:8132bccd-e900-4ba0-942e-c3114502bda2`) and a separate MATLAB GUI repo. No training required — it's a formula, portable to Python in the same style as `ssm_baselines.py`'s PET/DRAC. **Caveat**: the published constants were fit to n=1 human subject in one fixed-base simulator study (10 normal + 10 sport trials) — using them unmodified on Bench2Drive/DeepAccident is a reasonable "off-the-shelf analytic baseline" move (same spirit as using published PET/DRAC formulas), but should be reported as such, not as a properly-recalibrated risk model for these datasets. Needs one design decision not in the source paper: how to turn a continuous field value into a bounded [0,1] score comparable to PET/DRAC/R (mirroring `pet_score`/`drac_score`'s existing convention), and how to get the "predicted arc" input (turning-circle center/radius) from your trajectory adapters' current position+heading+curvature.

**#11 RiskNet (Liu et al. 2025) — feasible only as a reimplementation, not an exact reproduction; moderate-high effort.**
Read the full PDF (arXiv:2504.15541) directly, not just the abstract. Findings:
- The deterministic interaction-field/force part (Eqs. 1-11: pairwise interaction energy from mass/velocity/danger-type, force = energy/distance, Doppler-effect-derived directional weighting) is fully specified analytically — **but two of its own free constants, `k_j` (per-agent-type danger factor) and `C_j` (environmental constraint factor), are never assigned concrete numeric values anywhere in the paper**, only defined conceptually (`k_j ∈ [0,1]` for pedestrian/cyclist/car/truck). The lateral-decay constant β is given only as a range (0.5-2). A direct search of the full text for training hyperparameters (learning rate, epochs, batch size, optimizer) returned **zero hits** — none are reported.
- The probabilistic trajectory-prediction module is explicitly stated to be "inspired by the design principles of the MTP-GO model" (GNN + Neural ODE) — MTP-GO itself (Westny et al., IEEE T-IV 2023) **does have official public code**: [github.com/westny/mtp-go](https://github.com/westny/mtp-go) (PyTorch + PyTorch Geometric + PyTorch Lightning), which is a real, reusable head start for the learned half.
- No GitHub/code or supplementary-data release found for RiskNet itself (checked the full PDF text for "github"/"code available"/"repository" — no hits).
- Evaluated on highD/inD/rounD (drone top-down BEV trajectories) — structurally different from your Bench2Drive/DeepAccident adapters (3D oriented boxes from simulator/motion-data). Getting these datasets requires an academic license request via levelXdata/drone-dataset.com (free, but a request-and-wait process, not instant download), and building a new dataset adapter of comparable scope to the ones already in `adapters/` — your own README documents how much real engineering (bug-fixing, empirical verification) each existing adapter took, and there's no reason to expect this one would be cheaper.
- **Verdict**: you can build something *in the spirit of* RiskNet (MTP-GO's public code as trajectory-prediction backbone + your own values for `k_j`/`C_j`/β, retrained/run on your own datasets), but you cannot reproduce its reported numbers exactly — the two undocumented free constants and the absent training details make an exact reproduction impossible from the paper alone, and running it on a different dataset than highD/inD/rounD changes what's being measured anyway. If you want it as a real empirical baseline, budget it as a moderate-to-large sub-project (new adapter or same-dataset acquisition + backbone integration + your own constant choices, clearly labeled "our reimplementation of RiskNet's mechanism" in the paper) rather than a quick add. If you only need it for related-work positioning, the equations above are enough to describe and contrast the mechanism without implementing it.

**#7 "Beyond 1D and oversimplified kinematics" (Li et al. 2023/2024) — highest feasibility of the three checked; recommended as a real experiment.**
Read the full PDF (arXiv:2312.07019). Key finding: **this is a different flavor of "3D-TTC" than what's already in this codebase.** `geometry.compute_3d_ttc` is a post-hoc *oracle* measure — it reads the already-observed future ground-truth poses and finds when the oriented boxes actually overlap (correct for this project's post-hoc setting, but not something a real-time system could compute, since it peeks at the true future). Li et al.'s framework instead formalizes the classic, *deployable* notion of TTC: given only the current state `χ(0)` and a vehicle-movement ODE `χ̇=f(χ,u)` (Eq. 1), hold the control input constant (`u(t)=u(0)`, "inspired by conventional TTC" — their own words), forward-project the trajectory analytically (closed-form for LTI/linearized systems, Section 2.2), and solve for the first time the projected shape overlaps another vehicle/obstacle (Eq. 2-3). Section 4.1 derives exactly this for a 2-D kinematic bicycle model (Eqs. 42-49: state `χ=[x,y,θ,v]`, control `u=[δ,a]`) — the natural, fully-specified "2D-predictive-TTC" your evaluation currently lacks (`evaluation.raw_ttc_baseline_score` only ever wraps the *oracle* `compute_3d_ttc`, never a from-current-state predictive one). No code/data-availability statement found anywhere in the full text.
- **Why it's cheap here specifically**: (1) no free/uncalibrated constants like RiskNet's `k_j`/`C_j` — every quantity in Eqs. 42-49 is directly measurable from your existing trajectories (position, heading, speed by finite difference; steering angle `δ` from heading-rate + wheelbase `L` via Eq. 48); (2) no new dataset/license — runs directly on already-cached Bench2Drive/DeepAccident preprocessed instances; (3) the collision condition (Eq. 2) can reuse this project's *own* `geometry.rect_corners`/`sat_overlap` machinery unchanged, just fed forward-*simulated* boxes instead of observed ones; (4) for held-constant `(v,δ)`, the *exact* nonlinear solution is a constant-curvature circular arc (closed-form, no linearization error at all) — an even cleaner implementation than the paper's own linearized-LTI approximation, usable as the primary baseline with the paper's Eq. 50 linearization available as a literal fallback/cross-check.
- **Concrete plan**: new module (e.g. `futurerisk/forward_ttc_baseline.py`) computing, per (t, j): estimate `(v(t), δ(t))` from finite differences → forward-project ego's and agent j's circular-arc trajectory over the same `Δt` grid `compute_3d_ttc` already uses → run `sat_overlap` on the projected boxes → first-overlap time → wrap with the same clip-and-invert convention as `raw_ttc_baseline_score`. Register as a new baseline (e.g. `'forward_ttc_2d'`) in `evaluation.compare_methods` alongside the existing oracle `raw_ttc`, PET, DRAC, analytic, CARM.
- **A genuinely citable experiment falls out of this almost for free**: also compute the naive 1D-longitudinal-only version (constant velocity, ignore steering — classic Hayward TTC) on the same instances, and report the 1D-vs-2D discrepancy on Bench2Drive/DeepAccident the way Li et al. report it on their own data (they claim up to ~300% divergence for non-critical TTC, ~20% for critical/<1.5s TTC) — lets you both (a) directly cite and empirically cross-check their headline claim on your datasets, and (b) show CARM/the oracle 3D-TTC still separates further from *even the best rule-based forward-projected variant*, which is a stronger novelty argument than comparing only against the oracle baseline you already have.
- **One judgment call to make and document** (same spirit as the judgment calls already logged in `futurerisk/README.md`): `δ`/`a` estimated by finite-differencing noisy observed trajectories will themselves be noisy (especially `δ`, a second-derivative-like quantity from heading rate) — likely needs light smoothing, consistent with how `geometry.heading_from_positions` already handles low-speed heading noise.
- **Effort estimate**: low, roughly on the order of the PET/DRAC baselines already in `ssm_baselines.py` (low-to-moderate, ~1-2 days) — no new adapter, no external data, no free constants to guess.

Medians of 0.0% in three of four buckets mean the 1D/2D divergence is concentrated in a *minority* of instances (near-zero yaw rate makes the two formulas coincide, by construction) rather than universal -- an honest, expected nuance, not a null result (means are pulled up by a real, large-magnitude tail). Bench2Drive's critical bucket is the one case with a substantial non-zero *median* (18.8%), consistent with Bench2Drive's scenario design (cut-in/overtaking/roundabout -- curvature-heavy) versus DeepAccident's likely more highway/car-following-heavy mix.

**Important caveat -- Bench2Drive cross-domain AUROC/AUPRC=1.000 for both new methods is NOT a real finding.** It is a known, pre-existing tautology of this specific (label, score) pairing, not a bug introduced here: Bench2Drive's `geometric_collision_label` is itself built from the same SAT-overlap test (`geometry.boxes_overlap`) that any TTC-family method's delta=0 term trivially re-derives, so label=1 always implies tau=0 is achievable and label=0 never does, by construction -- perfect separation is guaranteed, not learned or measured. Confirmed this is pre-existing and not specific to the new baseline: the project's own existing `raw 3D-TTC` and `y_analytic` rows in `v1_full_eval.py`'s results show the identical AUROC=1.000/AUPRC=1.000 on this exact slice, while CARM (0.808), RSS (0.662), Lu et al. (0.990), and GSSM (0.817) -- methods that don't reuse that exact geometric primitive -- do not. Do not cite the new baseline's Bench2Drive cross-domain number as a real result; the DeepAccident in-domain (native-label) numbers above are the meaningful comparison.
